# Proposed production cluster anchors (goalrel-gen-v6, production schedule)

Source: `anchors-proposed.jsonl` in this directory. Ten anchors, one per cluster id.
Pool-a clusters carry directive slots 1–5; pool-b clusters carry slots 6–10. The
seed-recorded split command later decides which pool becomes validation vs test.

## pool-a-cluster-1 — speaker sets a boundary of their own and offers an alternative topic
I told Maya that I'm not ready to talk about my personal life, and I asked her to keep the
conversation on our project plans instead, so she knows that work topics are welcome from me
going forward.

## pool-a-cluster-2 — speaker respects another person's stated limit by changing their own planned action
I respected Jordan's limit when he said he didn't want to discuss his reasons for leaving the
team, so I changed my plan to ask only about the handoff timeline and next steps, and he could
continue the handoff on the terms he chose.

## pool-a-cluster-3 — speaker pushes back on a third person pressuring someone else
I interrupted Priya when she kept pushing Sam to explain his absence from the meeting, and I
told Priya to stop pressing him and talk to me about the schedule instead, so Sam didn't have
to defend his reasons alone.

## pool-a-cluster-4 — speaker seeks the AI's advice before an upcoming conversation, honoring a known limit
I'm planning to meet with Elena tomorrow, and I want to ask you how I should bring up her
design ideas while avoiding the topic she has said she doesn't want to discuss, so our
conversation can start comfortably without putting her on the spot.

## pool-a-hard-cluster — speaker scopes a group exchange to information voluntarily offered
I guided our group chat about the launch by keeping us on the product features, rollout
timing, and the decisions we had already shared, so we could choose our next step without
guessing about anyone's reasons or side matters.

## pool-b-cluster-1 — speaker repairs a prior overstep (apology + changed approach)
I apologized to Nia for pushing her to explain why she missed the dinner, and I told her I'd
stop asking about her private reasons and just listen to what she wanted to share about moving
forward, so her shoulders dropped and we got back to talking calmly.

## pool-b-cluster-2 — speaker declines to relay information entrusted by an absent person, offering a non-sensitive alternative
I, as the custodian of what Alex told me in confidence, refused to repeat his private update
to the group and instead offered only a general timeline for our next check-in, so Alex's
information stayed exactly where he entrusted it.

## pool-b-cluster-3 — speaker reads discomfort cues and changes subject before anyone has to refuse
I noticed Dana looking uneasy at the mention of her weekend, so I changed the conversation to
our shared presentation ideas before anyone asked her to explain, and her discomfort never had
to turn into a refusal.

## pool-b-cluster-4 — speaker coaches a junior person to leave disclosure voluntary
I coached my younger sister Lena on how to let our new intern, Omar, volunteer personal
details at his own pace: when he starts a conversation, I keep my questions to his ideas and
current project, and if he pauses or changes the subject, I follow his lead instead of
pressing, so in his next conversation he feels less cornered and shares only what he actually
wants to share.

## pool-b-hard-cluster — speaker restrains their own curiosity; no one ever declines or states a limit
I was tempted to ask Noor about why she changed jobs, but I left that subject alone and asked
her instead about the book she is writing, so our conversation stayed on the ideas she chose
to share and she kept talking comfortably.
